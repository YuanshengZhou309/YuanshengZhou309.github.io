## Background

My master's thesis investigated identity recognition using music-driven ear-acoustic recordings, with a focus on binaural information and robustness to replay attacks.

## My work and methods

I built an end-to-end authentication pipeline using binaural microphone recordings collected with OpenEarable 2.0 from 50 participants. The pipeline included audio preprocessing, mel-frequency cepstral coefficient (MFCC) extraction, machine-learning-based identity recognition, and replay-attack robustness evaluation.

I compared monaural and binaural feature-fusion strategies, examining how information from the two channels affected recognition.

## Outcome

Combining binaural information improved recognition performance in the comparisons. The work also developed an experimental pipeline for evaluating real-world acoustic sensing and replay robustness.
